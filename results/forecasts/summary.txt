The forecast points to three threat areas dominating activity by the end of the horizon. Flaws that let attackers run their own code remotely are the largest single category, at roughly 1,252 new vulnerabilities a month. Denial-of-service weaknesses are next at about 988 a month, and they are also the fastest-growing category, nearly doubling from 637. A broad catch-all group of miscellaneous vulnerabilities is third at roughly 551 a month, up from 278. SQL injection flaws are rising quickly too, from 353 to 548 a month. All activity figures are monthly counts of new vulnerabilities, not cumulative totals.

On money, the model projects total losses of about $156.3 billion across the full twelve-month horizon, not per month. The middle 90 percent of simulated outcomes runs from roughly $142.9 billion to $171.1 billion, so the range is fairly tight and the estimate reasonably stable. At the 95 percent confidence level, losses reach about $171.1 billion, with average losses in the worst 5 percent of cases near $175.6 billion. Professional services carries the heaviest exposure at about $57.1 billion, followed by manufacturing at $31.0 billion and finance at $24.4 billion.
