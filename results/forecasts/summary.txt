Over the next year, the forecast points to a heavy and rising wave of cyber threats, with remote code execution standing out as the most active category, projected at roughly 1,795 vulnerabilities per month. Denial of service attacks follow at about 911 per month, and SQL injection at around 611 per month. The fastest-growing categories are miscellaneous vulnerabilities, which climb from about 327 to 578 per month, denial of service rising from 733 to 911, and SQL injection increasing from 458 to 611. These activity figures are monthly counts, not cumulative.

In terms of financial impact, the total projected economic loss over the full 12-month horizon is about $137 billion on average, with a median near $137 billion as well. The range of outcomes is fairly tight: 90 percent of scenarios fall between roughly $128 billion and $148 billion, and the worst 5 percent exceed about $148 billion, with an average loss in that extreme tail near $153 billion. These are totals for the entire year, not monthly figures.

The sectors facing the highest exposure are professional services, with an average loss near $49 billion, manufacturing at about $28 billion, and finance at roughly $22 billion. Confidence in these numbers is moderate, given the relatively narrow spread around the central estimate, but the upper tail shows meaningful risk of larger losses.
