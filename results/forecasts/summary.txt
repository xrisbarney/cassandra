The forecast projects that over the next 12 months, total economic losses from cyber threats will average around 137 billion dollars, with a 90 percent chance of falling between roughly 128 billion and 148 billion dollars. The most severe tail risk, measured by the 95th percentile value-at-risk, is about 148 billion dollars, and the expected loss in that worst-case scenario is near 153 billion dollars. These are cumulative totals for the full year, not monthly figures.

In terms of threat activity, the highest monthly counts of new vulnerabilities are expected for remote code execution, with about 1,795 per month, followed by denial of service at 911 per month, and SQL injection at 611 per month. The fastest-growing threats over the forecast period are miscellaneous vulnerabilities, rising from 327 to 578 per month, denial of service from 733 to 911 per month, and SQL injection from 458 to 611 per month. These are monthly rates.

The economic sectors facing the greatest loss exposure are professional services, with an average loss of 49.4 billion dollars and a tail risk of 50.8 billion dollars; manufacturing, averaging 27.6 billion dollars with a tail risk of 31.6 billion dollars; and finance, averaging 21.7 billion dollars with a tail risk of 25.1 billion dollars. The range of outcomes is wide, reflecting significant uncertainty, but the central estimate is tightly clustered around the mean and median values.
